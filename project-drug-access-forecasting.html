Drug Access & Affordability Forecasting

Spend Per Prescription Across Four Drug Categories

Built on official Medicaid State Drug Utilization Data (2019-2024, quarterly). This is market-wide Medicaid data, a legitimate proxy for industry-level demand and cost trends, not any single company's own internal numbers. Full write-up, BigQuery pipeline, and forecasting code on GitHub →

Open dashboard on Tableau Public ↗

Recommendations

  1. Give weight-loss access a deliberate response (negotiated rates, patient assistance, a lower-cost formulary alternative), the "affordability improves on its own" assumption has been wrong for this category for five years running.
  2. Monitor weight loss closely for margin and access reasons alike, cost and demand are compounding in the same direction at once.
  3. Use sexual health as the reference case for what GLP-1 pricing could look like once a generic eventually arrives, so that shift is anticipated rather than a surprise.

Key Findings

Three of four categories got more affordable, weight loss didn't

Spend per prescription 2019-2024: sexual health -67%, hair loss +16%, mental health +18%, weight loss +39%, at the same time weight-loss prescription volume grew roughly 5x. No generic GLP-1s exist yet, so there's no organic price-relief mechanism working the way generic competition did for the other three.

Cost and demand are compounding in the same direction for weight loss

Rising cost per script and rising volume at the same time means total category spend is compounding on both fronts.

Forecasts were validated against held-out actuals, not just fitted

Trend/seasonality diagnosed per category before picking a method (Holt linear trend vs. Holt-Winters), time-based train/test split (never randomly shuffled for a time series), MAPE of 2.8-11.0% across categories.

Sexual health's affordability trend is a real pattern, not noise

Whatever drove that category's 67% cost decline (generic maturity, most likely) is a clear, sustained trend across the full time series, not a one-quarter blip.
