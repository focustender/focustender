Telehealth No-Show Prediction

SMS Reminders & the Accessibility Gap

Built on a public Kaggle dataset (110,521 Brazilian clinic appointments), used here to demonstrate a realistic telehealth/clinic scheduling analysis, not proprietary or production data. Full write-up and notebooks on GitHub →

Recommendations

  1. Shorten scheduling lead times where clinically reasonable, this moves no-show rates more than any reminder change could.
  2. Keep the SMS system, but close the income gap with something that addresses the underlying barrier (transportation, time off work) directly.
  3. Route the chronic no-show segment to direct case management instead of more SMS volume, standard reminders aren't reaching them.
  4. Prioritize chronic-condition patients in outreach anyway, a missed visit costs more when someone is managing a chronic condition, whatever their baseline odds of missing it.

Key Findings

Lead time is the dominant driver of no-shows

Same-day appointments no-show at just 4.6%, versus 24-33% for anything booked further in advance.

SMS works, but can't close the income gap on its own

Scholarship patients (a welfare-enrollment flag, the closest proxy this dataset has to income) no-show 4 points more than everyone else. SMS reduces no-shows by a similar percentage for both groups, tested directly with an interaction term rather than read off a crosstab, so the gap that remains isn't the reminder system failing low-income patients. It's a pre-existing difference an equal-percentage intervention structurally can't erase.

A small chronic no-show segment behaves differently

About 4% of repeat patients never show up at all, accounting for roughly 11% of every no-show in the dataset.

Chronic conditions looked protective, until age was controlled for

Hypertension and diabetes patients no-show less in a raw comparison, but that's an age confound (hypertension prevalence climbs from 0.1% at ages 0-17 to 61.6% at 75+), not the condition itself.
