The deliverable

Sample Kit Builder

The Trade Portal module built to close the single-sample dead end above: a companion-sample recommendation, live in a real Shopify theme.

Problem

73% of trade-show leads stall after exactly one sample. The strongest predictor of purchase is a second sample request, which trade-show leads make at less than half the rate of the trade segment overall.

Action

Built a companion-sample recommendation module that surfaces a second sample at the exact moment a trade lead requests their first. Taken to a build-ready Figma spec and a live Shopify theme section reading a real product metafield.

Tested live, not assumed

Before calling it done, I ran it against the live store and found the companion products had zero inventory with orders blocked, meaning it would have failed silently on a real lead's first click. Restocked live and rebuilt the cart-add as a real Shopify Ajax Cart API call, in place of the original stub. This is the same testing discipline behind every finding on this page: check the live system, don't trust the mockup.

Shipped, ready to measure

Live in a dev theme preview now. The metric it's built to move is the one this data shows actually predicts purchase: the second-sample-request rate.

Findings

Two more numbers worth the time

Conversion rate is not revenue

Designer
60.7%conversion, highest of any segment
$791revenue per customer
Trade
34.2%conversion, about half Designer's
$5,782revenue per customer, 7x Designer's

Designer customers convert best and spend least. Trade converts at half that rate and is worth seven times more. A program built around conversion rate alone optimizes for the wrong segment.

Two CRMs, one number they can't agree on

All 85 contacts genuinely live in both systems show this same split. One mechanism, a field that stopped updating after import, not scattered noise. The fix I scoped: one Salesforce Flow plus one HubSpot API call.

Killed the test before it wasted a quarter

623 leads needed · ~10 / month arrive · 62 months to get there

The backlog's own proposed A/B test needs 623 trade-show leads to reach significance. I killed the randomized-holdout design, not the hypothesis, and recommended a full rollout with two quarters of directional monitoring and a clear reversal bar instead.

Everything else

The rest of the process

This round also ran a formal data-quality audit (9.41 out of 10, zero critical findings), a cohort-retention analysis, a time-series check on the stall rate's trend, a root-cause drill-down that came back honestly inconclusive, a full semantic model, and a dashboard specification, twelve analyses in total. All of it real, all of it written up. None of it is hidden. It's just not the headline.
