Trade Signal

A Lifecycle Growth & Analytics Work Sample, Built for Fireclay Tile

Built as a self-directed work sample for Fireclay Tile's Lifecycle Growth & Analytics Manager opening — not affiliated with or endorsed by Fireclay Tile. All customer data is synthetic; the trade-show stall finding below is a property of the generated dataset, not a real Fireclay statistic. Full case study write-up →

The question behind this project: if Fireclay's own customer journey is homeowner → designer → trade → commercial, where does the biggest, most fixable stall actually sit, and can that finding survive being built on top of real tools instead of staying a slide? That meant generating a lifecycle dataset shaped like Fireclay's real segments, reconciling a deliberately messy CRM export the way a real one would arrive, seeding the result live into HubSpot, and carrying the resulting intervention (a Sample Kit Builder) all the way to a real Shopify theme section and a build-ready Figma spec, rather than stopping at a chart.

Key Findings

73% of trade-show-acquired leads stall after one sample

Breaking trade-segment single-sample requesters down by acquisition channel: 73% of leads acquired at trade shows who request exactly one sample never advance to a project upload or purchase — more than double the stall rate of leads from email capture (33.3%). Trade-show leads are also disproportionately unlikely to request a second sample (11%, versus 22% of trade leads overall) — the single point a Sample Kit Builder / Trade Portal intervention should target first, since a second sample request is the strongest observed predictor of eventual purchase in this segment.

A naive CRM cleanup would have hidden 34 of those customers entirely

The raw CRM export backing this analysis is deliberately messy: inconsistent segment casing, mixed date formats, and duplicate-ish rows split across multiple partial records per customer. Reconciling it by grouping raw rows per customer and coalescing fields — rather than a naive first-row-wins dedupe — took 669 raw rows down to 600 distinct customers and recovered acquisition channel for 34 customers who would otherwise have been reported as unattributed, silently darkening the channel-level finding above.

Every contact shared between HubSpot and Salesforce has a mismatched lifecycle stage

Reconciling the two live, real accounts against each other (not a mockup of one) found all 85 contacts genuinely present in both systems still showing HubSpot's lifecyclestage stuck at "lead" while Salesforce already reads "Closed - Converted" — one mechanism (a field never written to after import), not scattered noise. The fix is scoped to one Salesforce Flow and one HubSpot API call, one direction only, not a platform purchase.

Experimentation

The proposed test can't reach significance

A power analysis on the growth backlog's own proposed test — a 90-day holdout measuring an 8-point-scale threshold — shows it needs 623 trade-show leads. The channel produces roughly 10 a month, putting that 62 months out. A faster-arriving proxy metric doesn't fix it either, confirming a real population ceiling rather than a fixable test design.

Stop the holdout, not the hypothesis

Instead: full rollout of the Sample Kit Builder nudge to every eligible trade-show lead, directional monitoring over two quarters against the 11% baseline second-sample rate, and an explicit reversal bar — pull back if the rate doesn't land at least 5 points above baseline.
