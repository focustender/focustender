Facilities Maintenance Analytics

Reactive vs. Proactive Analysis

This project uses fully synthetic data. It's built to statistically match a real facilities-maintenance operation, but every building, technician, and individual work order in the dataset is generated, not extracted from actual company records. Full write-up, code, and data-handling details on GitHub →

The goal was to separate a genuine reliability problem from a volume problem, and figure out whether the maintenance backlog was a technician-performance issue or a building- and trade-specific one. The business metrics in view were on-time completion rate and workload distribution across technicians, aimed at surfacing real workflow and staffing problem areas before recommending a proactive-maintenance program instead of just adding headcount.

Recommendations

  1. Target the 8-building watch list directly, a blanket "add more staff" response would miss them entirely.
  2. Review staffing, parts, and vendor contracts for Mechanical/Utility trades specifically before assuming a broader fix is needed.
  3. Point any performance conversation at buildings and a trade, not individual technicians.
  4. Review staffing capacity and rebalance workload across the team, the current concentration on 4 of 9 technicians is a burnout and single-point-of-failure risk.
  5. Pilot formalized technician-building assignments so technicians build equipment familiarity over time, a forward-looking proposal, not a pattern the data proved.
  6. Use this six-month window as the "before" baseline to measure a future proactive program against.

Key Findings

Building volume and reliability are not the same problem

The two busiest buildings run a normal 18-20% late rate. A separate group of 8 buildings, only 13% of total volume, runs late 42-85% of the time.

One trade is driving the watch list

Mechanical/Utility Systems work is overrepresented on the watch list by 11-13 percentage points, the largest gap of any category.

Not a technician performance problem

On-time rates cluster tightly across the whole team (13.6-23.4%), no individual stands out.

Workload is concentrated on 4 of 9 technicians

Four technicians carry roughly double the workload of the other five, in both task count and actual minutes worked, not a counting artifact.

Worth piloting: formalized technician-building assignments

A standard proactive-maintenance lever, pairing technicians consistently with the same buildings so they build equipment familiarity over time. Framed as a forward-looking proposal, not a pattern this dataset proved, whether consistent assignment already happens informally is a real open question the synthetic data can't answer.

This dataset is the "before" baseline

Late rate holds flat around 20-21% every month, no proactive program exists yet.
